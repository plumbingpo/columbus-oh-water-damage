How Toledo Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Most of the water damage we see in Toledo homes started with something small that gave warning first. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Start with supply lines. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. Check the water heater for rust at the base and drain a little from the tank once a year to clear sediment. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. If you find something in your Toledo home that worries you, such as a stain that keeps growing, have it looked at before it becomes a cleanup.

Common Water Damage Issues in Toledo

In Toledo, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers all depend on small hoses that wear out. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Missing shingles, cracked flashing and clogged gutters let rain into the attic. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. The crew from Columbus Restoration Co can dry the attic and ceiling, but the roof itself needs a repair to stop the leak.

Basement: In homes that have a basement, groundwater pushes through foundation cracks and floor joints when the soil is saturated. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. Many mold jobs we handle in Toledo began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
